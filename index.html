How do I know if I owe enough?

Grab last year's Ohio IT 1040 and find line 10 on page 2, "Tax liability after nonrefundable credits." If it's at least $750 ($1,500 for a married couple filing jointly), the full Ohio credit fits. For the federal credit, check the tax on your federal Form 1040. Many retirees owe less than you'd think, so check before you give.

You give first. The credit comes back when you file your taxes, as a smaller tax bill or a bigger refund. Bloom Ohio isn't a tax advisor, so talk with your tax professional about your situation.

From your gift to a student's desk

For families

Hoping for a scholarship for your child?

Bloom Ohio scholarships help families at our 11 schools close the gap between tuition and what they can pay. You don't need to be Adventist. Awards are based on need.

Who can apply

  • Students enrolled or accepted at one of our 11 schools
  • Families with incomes up to 300% of the federal poverty level are served first
  • Then families up to 300% of their area's median income
  • Last year's recipients and their siblings get priority

How awards work

  • Your award fills the gap after EdChoice and what your family can pay
  • It covers tuition, required fees, books and uniforms
  • Money goes straight to your school account
  • Bloom Ohio pays the FACTS application fee if you need help

Key dates

  • Jan 1 to Mar 31, 2027: apply through your school's Bloom Ohio contact
  • Apr 16 to May 31: awards decided
  • Aug 15 and Jan 15: paid to your school
  • First awards are for the 2027-28 school year

Get ready now

  • Keep your 2026 tax return handy
  • Know your EdChoice status
  • Ask your principal who your Bloom Ohio contact is
  • Join the interest list to get the application the day it opens

Quick answers

The questions people ask first

Can my business use the credit?

The credits belong to people, not companies. A business owner can give personally and claim the credits on their own return, and a spouse can do the same. Owners of an LLC, S-corp or partnership can share up to $750 of Ohio credit from a business gift. Businesses can't use the federal credit. The best thing a business can do is give personally and tell its employees.

Is anything above the credit tax-deductible?

Any part of your gift that doesn't earn a credit may be deductible as a charitable gift if you itemize. Bloom Ohio's 501(c)(3) application is with the IRS now. Your tax professional can tell you what applies to you.

Who runs Bloom Ohio, and who checks the books?

Bloom Ohio is a nonprofit created by the Ohio Conference of Seventh-day Adventists for this one purpose. At least 90% of its income goes to scholarships. Its money sits in its own separate account. An independent audit runs every year, and a public report shows what was raised and how many students were helped. A seven-member committee that includes church members from across Ohio picks recipients by need.

When do I get my money back?

You give first. The credit shows up when you file your taxes for that year, as a smaller tax bill or a bigger refund. Ohio lets gifts made by the April tax deadline count toward the year before.

Where can I check the official rules?

The Ohio Department of Taxation explains the state credit at tax.ohio.gov. Read the Ohio scholarship donation credit page.

Bloom Ohio isn't a tax advisor. Talk with your tax professional about your situation. Credits are dollar for dollar but nonrefundable, so they can only reduce tax you actually owe. The Ohio credit (ORC 5747.73) is up to $750 per person and $1,500 per married couple, with each spouse giving separately. The federal Education Freedom Tax Credit (IRC 25F) begins January 1, 2027: up to $1,700 per person after the Ohio credit, under Treasury and IRS proposed regulations released October 1, 2026 that may still change. Federal-credit gifts must be marked as such when you give. Credits are for cash gifts only. Gifts can't be designated for a specific student. An initiative of the Ohio Conference Office of Education.
